# Complete Narrative Architecture and Chronological Deconstruction

## 1. Introduction: Fabula vs. Syuzhet

To analyze *The Last of Us Part II* (Naughty Dog, 2020) with narratological rigor, we must deploy the classical formalist distinction formulated by Gérard Genette (1980) between:
- **Fabula (Story / Chronology):** The chronological sequence of events as they objectively occur in the fictional reality.
- **Syuzhet (Plot / Discourse):** The artistic arrangement, omissions, anachronies (analepses/flashbacks and prolepses/flashforwards), and perspective shifts through which the events are revealed to the player.

The emotional power and moral discomfort of *The Last of Us Part II* are direct products of the radical divergence between its *fabula* and its *syuzhet*. By strictly controlling the temporal distribution of information, Naughty Dog first weaponizes the player's grief and filial devotion to Joel to fuel a violent revenge quest, only to retroactively undermine that mission by unveiling the suppressed fabula from the perspective of Joel's executioners.

## 3. Syuzhet: Structural Discourse and Controlled Information

The game’s syuzhet is structured into four overarching structural movements designed to systematically manipulate player alignment:

```
Movement I: Attachment & Retribution    ──> Movement II: Reversal & Humanization
(Prologue & Ellie's Seattle Days 1–3)       (The Midpoint Reset & Abby's Days 1–3)
             │                                           │
             ▼                                           ▼
Movement III: The Climax at the Theater ──> Movement IV: Exhaustion & Catharsis
(Forced Protagonist Battle)                 (The Farmhouse, Santa Barbara, & The Porch)
```

### Movement I: The Architecture of Grievance (Prologue & Ellie Days 1–3)
1. **The Epistemological Framing:** The syuzhet begins not with Joel’s violence, but with Joel’s confession to Tommy about saving Ellie from the hospital, followed immediately by Joel gifting Ellie the guitar and singing "Future Days." This reinforces the foundational paternal mythos of the first game.
2. **The Trauma Catalyst:** Joel’s murder at the hands of Abby is staged with horrifying intimacy. The player is forced into Ellie’s immobilized perspective—pinned to the floor, forced to watch Joel’s skull crushed by a golf club. Crucially, the narrative deliberately *withholds* Abby’s identity, her surname, and her motive. To the player and Ellie, Abby is an irrational, sadistic monster.
3. **The Righteous Crusade:** Ellie’s Seattle Days 1–3 are constructed as a righteous detective pursuit. The player methodically hunts down Abby’s accomplices (Jordan, Nick, Leah, Nora, Owen, Mel).
4. **The Somatic Degradation:** While the player believes they are pursuing justice, Ellie’s interiority visibly deteriorates:
   - **Nora’s Torture:** In the red-lit basement of the Seattle hospital, the player is forced to repeatedly press the square button to beat a spore-infected Nora with a pipe. When Ellie returns to the theater, her hands are shaking uncontrollably, her trauma manifesting as somatic tremors.
   - **Mel’s Corpse:** When Ellie shoots Owen and stabs Mel in self-defense at the aquarium, she opens Mel’s jacket to discover she was heavily pregnant. Ellie experiences acute hyperventilation and moral nausea; the righteous veneer of revenge completely shatters.

### Movement II: The Midpoint Inversion (Abby Days 1–3)
At the pinnacle of dramatic tension—Abby kicks open the theater door, shoots Jesse through the face, holds Tommy hostage, and aims her gun at Ellie—the screen cuts abruptly to black.

Instead of resolving the cliffhanger, the narrative rewinds three days:
- **Title Card:** *Seattle Day 1 — Abby*.
- **The Epistemological Shock:** The player is forced to control the woman they have spent 15 hours hunting. 
- **Retrospective Recontextualization:**
  - The player discovers that Jerry Anderson—the unnamed NPC doctor Joel slaughtered in the first game's climax—was Abby’s beloved father, a compassionate biologist attempting to synthesize a vaccine to save humanity.
  - The Seattle hospital, aquarium, and streets previously traversed as Ellie are revisited, revealing that the "faceless WLF grunts" and "sadistic conspirators" Ellie slaughtered were young people with romantic entanglements, moral doubts, domestic routines, and companion animals (such as Alice the dog, whom Ellie was forced to stab).
- **The Parallel Arc:** Abby’s three days mirror Ellie’s, but in reverse moral trajectory. While Ellie descends from light into darkness (consuming herself with hatred), Abby attempts to ascend from darkness into redemption. Having already achieved her revenge against Joel—and finding that it cured none of her nightmares—Abby risks her life, betrays her faction, and slaughters her former WLF comrades to protect two enemy Seraphite children: Yara and Lev.

### Movement III: The Climax at the Theater (The Inhabited Double-Bind)
When the narrative returns to the theater confrontation, the player does not resume control of Ellie. In a move of unprecedented ludic daring, **the game forces the player to control Abby as she hunts down Ellie**.
- The player must stalk Ellie through the dark backstage, dodging Ellie’s trip-mines, shotgun blasts, and smoke bombs.
- This creates profound *ludo-affective dissonance*: the player is forced to brutalize the protagonist they have loved for two games.
- When Abby pins a pregnant Dina to the floor and prepares to slit her throat with a combat knife ("She's pregnant!" / "Good."), only Lev’s voice ("Abby!") halts the murder. Abby spares them, establishing Lev as her moral conscience.

### Movement IV: Santa Barbara and the Delayed Porch Scene (Catharsis)
1. **The Farmhouse Illusion:** Ellie attempts domestic pastoral life with Dina and baby JJ, but her PTSD is clinical and somatic. The sudden clatter of a falling shovel in the sheep barn plunges her into sensory paralysis.
2. **Santa Barbara:** When Tommy brings word of Abby’s whereabouts, Ellie departs not out of bloodlust, but out of a desperate, broken attempt to stop the psychic hemorrhaging of her trauma.
3. **The Crucifixion at the Pillars:** Abby is discovered not as a triumphant warrior, but emaciated, sun-bleached, and tied to a wooden pillar by the Rattlers, stripped of her physical muscularity.
4. **The Final Drowning:** Ellie cuts Abby down, forces her into a fistfight in the shallow surf, and begins drowning her. As Abby’s struggles weaken, the game flashes an image of Joel’s bloody, pulverized face.
5. **The Memory Breakthrough:** At the threshold of Abby’s death, the intrusive traumatic image of Joel’s battered skull is suddenly replaced by a serene image: Joel sitting on his porch with his guitar in the evening light. Ellie releases Abby: "Just take him."
6. **The Epilogue & Porch Conversation:** Returning to the abandoned farmhouse, Ellie attempts to play Joel's guitar, but her missing fingers (bitten off by Abby) render her unable to form the chords of "Future Days." The narrative finally reveals the complete porch conversation from the night before Joel died:
   - Ellie confronted Joel about taking away her choice to die for the vaccine. Joel replied: *"If somehow the Lord gave me a second chance at that moment... I would do it all over again."*
   - Ellie responded: *"I don't think I can ever forgive you for that. But I would like to try."*
   - **The Final Hermeneutic Revelation:** Ellie’s agonizing rage was never just about Joel’s death; it was about the fact that Abby stole her chance to forgive him. By sparing Abby, Ellie honors the nascent impulse of forgiveness she extended to Joel on that porch, breaking the cycle of retribution and leaving the guitar behind to begin authentic mourning.
