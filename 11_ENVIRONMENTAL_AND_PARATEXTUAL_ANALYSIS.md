# Environmental Storytelling, Paratextual Narratives, and World-Building

## 1. Theoretical Grounding: Paratextuality and Spatial Narrative

In video game theory, narrative is not confined to linear cutscenes and spoken dialogue; it is embedded within the material contours of the virtual environment and its tactile artifacts. Drawing upon **Gérard Genette’s (1997)** conceptualization of *paratexts*—thresholds of interpretation that mediate between the reader and the core text—and **Ryan Banfi’s (2022)** analysis of intradiegetic artifacts, this chapter investigates how *The Last of Us Part II* deploys **spatial storytelling, found notes, and Ellie’s personal journal** as critical narrative instruments.

Furthermore, integrating **Valentina Romanzi’s (2023)** sociological critique of post-apocalyptic frontier structures, we examine how the game’s physical environments map competing ideological models of human organization in the wake of societal collapse.

---

## 2. Ellie’s Journal as an Intradiegetic Para-Narrative

One of the most sophisticated narratological devices in *Part II* is Ellie’s personal sketchbook and diary, accessible at any point by the player pressing the touchpad. As Banfi (2022) demonstrates, the journal operates as an **essential para-narrative** that systematically deconstructs Ellie’s outward stoicism:

```
                            ELLIE'S DUAL REGISTER
                                      │
       ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
       ▼                                                             ▼
[Spoken Dialogue (Public Performative)]                       [Journal Entries (Private Interiority)]
- Cynical, tough, emotionally guarded.                       - Visceral terror, poetic fragility, weeping.
- Rigid commitment to vengeance ("We kill them").            - Acute self-doubt ("What am I doing here?").
- Dismissive of personal pain with Dina/Jesse.               - Somatic recording of tremors, panic, and nausea.
- Suppresses mention of Joel to avoid tears.                 - Sketches of Joel's eyes, repeatedly erased.
```

### The Unreachable Eyes of Joel
The most heartbreaking visual motif within the journal is Ellie’s repeated, failed attempts to sketch Joel's face from memory:
- Following the Jackson murder, Ellie attempts to draw Joel in her journal, but her charcoal strokes constantly scratch out his eyes, tearing through the paper.
- Traumatized by the visual memory of his battered face in the basement, her mind cannot reconstruct his living, loving visage.
- It is only in the epilogue—after sparing Abby on the beach and breaking the compulsive cycle of trauma—that she is finally able to draw Joel sitting peacefully on his porch with his guitar, his eyes open, clear, and serene. The journal visually documents the psychological transition from traumatic fragmentation to symbolic resolution.

### Hidden Psychological Confessions
The journal reveals vital interior data that Ellie deliberately conceals from her companions:
- Following the torture of Nora, Ellie does not speak to Dina of her moral horror; but in her journal, she writes broken, shaking fragments: *"I can still hear her screaming. My hands won't stop shaking. What is wrong with me?"*
- During the farmhouse chapter, while Dina believes Ellie is adjusting to peaceful agrarian life, Ellie’s diary is filled with terrifying poetry about suffocating in blood, accompanied by frantic sketches of gallows and wolves, exposing that her domestic tranquility is a fragile façade.

## 3. Sociopolitical Topography: The Four Ideological Landscapes

Following Romanzi (2023), the spatial environments traversed across the American West represent distinct sociological responses to the collapse of the state:

```
                                SOCIOPOLITICAL TOPOGRAPHY
                                            │
        ┌───────────────────┬───────────────┴───────────────┬───────────────────┐
        ▼                   ▼                               ▼                   ▼
    [Jackson]         [WLF: Seattle]               [Seraphites: Haven]      [Rattlers: SB]
Egalitarian Civic   Militarized Fascist           Religious Agrarian       Exploitative Slaver
Communalism         Totalitarianism               Fundamentalism           Decadence
```

### A. Jackson, Wyoming: Civic Communalism and Agrarian Social Trust
Jackson represents the sole functioning utopian space in the franchise:
- **Spatial Architecture:** A fortified mountain town powered by a restored hydroelectric dam. The streets feature greenhouse gardens, a communal laundry, a functional public school, warm taverns, and thriving craft workshops.
- **Sociopolitical Structure:** Governed by an elected municipal council led by Maria Miller. Resources are distributed on an egalitarian basis; labor is shared collectively; children and the elderly are shielded from frontier brutality.
- **Thematic Function:** Proves that the apocalypse does not inevitably produce savagery. Civilized humanity can regenerate when founded upon mutual aid, communal trust, and the rejection of militarized paranoia.

### B. The Washington Liberation Front (WLF): Totalitarian Militarism
The WLF (the "Wolves") embodies the perversion of democratic liberation into fascist military dictatorship:
- **Spatial Architecture:** Having overthrown the authoritarian military regime of FEDRA in Seattle, the WLF established their headquarters within CenturyLink Field (the Seattle Seahawks stadium).
- **The Bureaucracy of War:** The stadium has been transformed into a terrifyingly efficient, industrialized military machine:
  - The football field is partitioned into high-yield livestock pastures and corn crops.
  - The luxury suites are converted into uniform barracks and armories.
  - The lower concourses house dog-breeding kennels, weapons-testing firing ranges, and administrative offices where logistics boards track kill-counts and ammunition quotas.
- **Ideological Function:** Under the leadership of Isaac Dixon, the WLF demonstrates that an obsession with security, territorial expansion, and retributive violence against the Seraphites eventually consumes the very humanity the faction sought to protect.

### C. The Seraphites (Scars): Religious Fundamentalism and Luddite Purity
In radical contrast to the industrialized WLF, the Seraphites represent a neo-primitive, theocratic cult:
- **Spatial Architecture:** Inhabiting the heavily forested, isolated Haven island off the coast of flooded Seattle, the Seraphites have rejected old-world technology, electricity, and firearms. Their settlements consist of intricately carved wooden longhouses, prayer bridges, and candlelit shrines dedicated to their deceased female "Prophet."
- **Communication and Ritual:** They communicate across battlefields through an elaborate system of melodic acoustic whistles, stripping language of decipherable meaning for outsiders.
- **Theocratic Oppression:** While their communal villages exhibit ecological harmony and peaceful agrarian crafts, their social structure is violently dogmatic: apostates are ritually disemboweled and hung from lampposts, and young girls are forcibly assigned as child-brides to elderly male elders.

### D. The Rattlers: Santa Barbara and the Depravity of Slaver Decadence
The final faction encountered in the sunny coastal sprawl of Santa Barbara represents the absolute nadir of human degeneration:
- **Spatial Architecture:** Headquartered in an opulent, sun-drenched Spanish-colonial resort compound surrounded by electrified chain-link fences.
- **The Economy of Enslavement:** The Rattlers are a heavily armed syndicate of sadistic slavers who capture wandering survivors and force them into brutal agricultural chain-gangs.
- **The Weaponization of the Infected:** The Rattlers keep clickers and runners chained to iron posts like vicious junkyard dogs, laughing as they use human beings for target practice or torture. They embody pure nihilistic sadism—survival stripped of all moral dignity, existing purely to exploit the vulnerable.

## 4. Micro-Narratives: Environmental Artifacts and the Voices of the Dead

Beyond macro-factions, the game’s world is saturated with dozens of self-contained, micro-narratives told through abandoned spaces, environmental staging, and handwritten letters. These artifacts reinforce the central thesis that **every human being who perished had a life, a family, and an unfinished story**:

| Found Artifact / Location | Micro-Narrative Summary | Thematic & Hermeneutic Resonance |
|---|---|---|
| **Boris and the Hillcrest Uprising** (Seattle Day 2) | Notes found in Hillcrest track Boris, a renowned local archer whose teenage daughter Sofia was executed by the WLF for spray-painting slogans. Driven mad by grief, Boris laced his neighborhood with tripwires and lured both WLF patrols and infected into traps, eventually turning into a stalker in his own garage. | Prefigures Ellie's trajectory: a parent driven to psychotic self-destruction and lethal obsession by the murder of a child. |
| **The Bank Heist Gone Wrong** (Downtown Seattle) | Inside the ruins of the Westlake Bank, Ellie discovers the skeletal remains of robbers who were trapped in the vault during Outbreak Day, surrounding bags of useless paper currency. | Satirical commentary on the absolute worthlessness of pre-apocalyptic capital in the face of biological catastrophe. |
| **Paige and the Serevena Hotel** (Seattle Day 1) | Letters between WLF scouts trapped in the Serevena Hotel document their slow starvation, panic over infected swarms, and agonizing farewells to families in the stadium. | Strips the WLF of their "faceless enemy" status before Ellie slaughters their survivors in the upper suites. |
| **The Hospital Pediatrics Ward** (Abby Day 2) | While descending through the Seattle hospital to Ground Zero, Abby discovers crayon drawings by dying children quarantined in 2013, alongside desperate logs from nurses trying to comfort terrified parents. | Reconnects Abby (and the player) to the founding humanitarian mission of the medical profession, elevating her quest to save Yara. |
| **The Aquarium Sailor's Journal** (Abby Day 1) | Owen discovers the journal of Max, an elderly sailor who lived alone in the aquarium for twenty years, keeping the sea lion exhibits clean until he quietly died in his bed. | Inspires Owen’s dream that beauty, peace, and restorative care can exist outside of militarized factions. |

## 5. Synthesis: The World as a Moral Witness

The physical world of *The Last of Us Part II* does not serve as a passive backdrop for protagonist action; it acts as an **active moral witness**. 

Nature is shown vigorously reclaiming the crumbling concrete monuments of human arrogance: moss blankets luxury shopping centers, ferns crack through paved highways, and water lilies bloom in flooded streets. The ruins of Seattle demonstrate that nature is completely indifferent to the ideological wars of human beings. By placing the petty, vindictive bloodletting of the WLF, Seraphites, Ellie, and Abby within this vast, reclaimed landscape, the environmental design silently conveys the ultimate tragedy of their struggle: **human beings tearing each other to pieces over historical grievances in a world that has already moved on without them**.
